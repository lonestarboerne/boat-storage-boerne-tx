Do You Need Special Insurance to Store a Boat in Boerne?

Insurance is one of those details that's easy to assume is handled and never actually checked. A lot of boat owners store their vehicle for months at a time without confirming what their policy actually covers while it's sitting in a unit rather than on the water, and that gap is exactly when an uncovered claim tends to surface.

Storage Coverage Isn't Automatic

Most boat insurance policies are built around use, coverage for accidents while operating the boat. Whether that same policy covers theft, fire, or weather damage while the boat is parked in storage depends entirely on the specific policy, and it's not something to assume without checking directly with an insurance provider. Some policies include storage coverage automatically; others require an add-on or a separate rider.

What to Actually Ask Your Insurer

The choice between covered and enclosed storage isn't purely about cost, it can affect insurance too. Fully enclosed, garage-style units with lighting and electric, running $249 for 12.5'x30' and $299 for 12.5'x40', shield a boat from the weather-related damage that tends to generate claims in the first place. Documenting What's in Storage

Insurance claims move faster with documentation, and this matters more for a boat that isn't checked on daily. Taking photos before it goes into storage, keeping a copy of the policy accessible, and knowing the exact unit location and facility address ahead of time all make a potential claim process smoother if something ever does happen.

Battery-related claims: A less obvious insurance angle, dead or damaged batteries from months of disconnected storage can sometimes complicate a claim if a boat won't start during an inspection or sea trial after a covered event.
